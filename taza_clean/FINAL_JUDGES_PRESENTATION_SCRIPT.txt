5. FRONTEND & CLOUD CI/CD DEPLOYMENT:

"Our backend follows a robust Clean Layered Architecture divided into four tiers:

1. API GATEWAY & ROUTING LAYER (app/routers/):
   - Modular RESTful routers partitioned by domain:
     * /auth: Farmer and consumer phone OTP & credential authentication.
     * /farmers: Harvest listings, farmgate orders, and farmer dashboard.
     * /crops: Searchable consumer catalog with live decay ratings.
     * /orders: Dynamic weight multi-item orders, escrow & waybill tracking.
     * /logistics: Multi-stop route calculation and carbon emissions.
     * /weather: Satellite alerts and disaster mitigation advisories.
     * /analytics: District-level production and mandi price telemetry.

2. SECURITY & ROLE-BASED ACCESS CONTROL (app/core/deps.py & security.py):
   - Stateless cryptographic JWT Bearer tokens enforcing strict RBAC 
     separation between Farmers, Consumers, Transporters, and Admins.

3. VALIDATION & CONTRACT LAYER (app/schemas/):
   - Decouples data transport objects from database persistence, ensuring 
     complete payload integrity and data sanitization.

4. DOMAIN SERVICES & BUSINESS LOGIC LAYER (app/services/):
   - The computational brain where all AI models, route planning algorithms, 
     and pricing engines execute."

SECTION 4: AI & INTELLIGENT ALGORITHMIC CAPABILITIES (2:30 - 4:00)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"TAZA does not use static e-commerce rules. We have built 4 core AI & algorithmic 
engines into our backend:

1. BIOLOGICAL FRESHNESS DECAY AI ENGINE (pricing_freshness_service.py):
   - Farm produce decays continuously post-harvest. Our engine implements an 
     exponential half-life decay formulation:
     
         Freshness(t) = 100 * exp(-lambda * t)
         
     where lambda is the crop-specific biological constant (e.g., lambda=0.035 
     for fragile leafy greens like Lau Shak, versus lambda=0.012 for resilient 
     tubers like Chandramukhi & Jyoti Potato). It dynamically categorizes harvests 
     into 'Ultra Farm-Fresh (<6h)', 'Crisp & Fresh (<24h)', or 'Immediate-Cook Grade'.

2. REAL-TIME MANDI ARBITRAGE & FAIR-PRICING ENGINE:
   - Cross-references live APMC Mandi wholesale benchmark prices from official 
     Agmarknet records. It computes dynamic farmgate prices that guarantee 
     farmers earn 15%–25% above raw mandi realization, while consumers save 
     15%–30% compared to middleman retail rates, capped at a transparent 
     2% fair-trade platform fee.

3. GREEN ROUTE OPTIMIZATION & TSP SOLVER (logistics_service.py):
   - Solves the Traveling Salesperson Problem for multi-farm batch pickups using 
     a Greedy Nearest-Neighbor Algorithm with a 1.28x road tortuosity factor 
     for Bengal highways. It matches orders with green vehicle fleets (Electric 
     E-Rickshaws for <=15km and Tata Ace EVs for bulk loads), calculating 
     real-time carbon offsets.

4. SATELLITE AGRO-METEOROLOGY & DISASTER EARLY WARNING (weather_service.py):
   - Ingests high-resolution satellite weather data across 23 districts in West 
     Bengal. When precipitation risk exceeds 65%, it generates automated alerts 
     warning farmers to postpone harvesting moisture-sensitive crops (like potatoes) 
     and equips delivery vehicles with covered tarpaulins.

5. PERISHABLES VS STAPLES (RICE & PULSES AI MODELING):
   - For staple crops like Gobindobhog Rice, Minikit Rice, Sona Moong Dal, and 
     Musur Dal, the AI applies a Grain Longevity Model (lambda=0.0005, 1-year 
     shelf life), monitors relative humidity thresholds (>75%) to prevent fungal 
     mold, and automatically triggers high-payload EV transport for 5kg-50kg bulk sacks."

Q1: Why did you choose FastAPI over Django or Node.js?
A1: "FastAPI natively leverages Python’s ASGI standard (async/await) and uvloop, 
     allowing us to handle thousands of concurrent I/O operations (like multi-vendor 
     order placement, payment webhooks, and live tracking) with minimal memory 
     footprint, while Pydantic gives us compile-time schema validation."

Q2: How do you handle race conditions when two consumers order the last 5 kg of a crop?
A2: "We execute atomic MongoDB decrement operations ($inc: { quantity_available_kg: -qty }) 
     guarded by condition checks (quantity_available_kg >= requested_qty). If stock 
     drops below zero, the transaction fails immediately before payment initialization."

Q3: How does the pricing benefit the farmer if consumers pay less?
A3: "By eliminating the 5-7 middlemen who take 45% of the margin, we share that 
     saved value directly: the farmer gets 15-25% more than Mandi rates, and the 
     consumer pays 15-30% less, with the platform charging a tiny 2% fair-trade fee."

Q4: How do you handle network drops in rural Bengal?
A4: "Our backend implements the Circuit Breaker Pattern. If external APIs time out, 
     local mathematical fallbacks (Haversine spatial matrices and cached agro-climatic 
     indices) execute smoothly without failing the transaction."

Q5: How is the multi-item cart handled in the backend?
A5: "Each cart item creates an independent tracking order and waybill via concurrent 
     Promise.all async calls, enabling different farmers across Bengal to fulfill 
     their respective portions asynchronously."
